## 5. Interpretation

1. **Removing all latent translators does not hurt, and slightly helps PSNR.**
   - The 22.5M encoder–decoder is non-inferior to the 44.4M DSLT-A on every severity and metric under the pre-set margins.
   - It is formally equivalent for SSIM (mild, medium) and LPIPS (mild, severe).
   - Its PSNR is better at medium (+0.075 dB) and severe (+0.137 dB; 95% CI excludes 0; all seeds positive; Holm p = 8e-08).
   - It needs 49% fewer parameters and 39% fewer MACs, with 35% lower latency and 58% higher throughput.
2. **This agrees with Stage 1.** Switching translators off at inference cost ≤ 0.1 dB, and the trained architecture fully recovers that. The translators are not needed for restoration quality under this protocol.
3. **The e2-only variant offers no advantage over either.** It is inconclusive against `Tfull`, with high variance across seeds, and is dominated by `Tnone` on both cost and point estimates.
4. **Combined findings (Stages 0–2):** within this training budget, neither of DSLT's defining components helps, and both cost compute.
   - Damage-conditioned gating: Stage 0.
   - Latent translators: Stages 1–2.
   - A plain encoder–decoder with skip connections and a global residual is the most efficient configuration and at least as accurate.

## 6. Implications for the paper

- **What it can't claim:** the "DSLT" method claim (latent translation plus damage gating as the contribution) isn't supported.
- **What it can claim** (Direction A, now sharper): a controlled, statistically rigorous component study of a multi-severity restoration network, with these findings:
  1. A plain encoder–decoder matches or beats the full model at about half the cost.
  2. Latent translation modules are redundant.
  3. Damage-conditioned gating does not help, even with oracle severity (upper bound < 0.1 dB).
  4. Unsupervised gates collapse; supervised gates track damage but are not used.
- **Recommended naming and positioning:** present the encoder–decoder as the main model, and DSLT-A and the gated variants as ablations. Drop "latent translation" from the method name.

## 7. Limitations

1. **Budget:** 800 steps at 128 px is far from convergence. Translators or gating could matter more at convergence, and this is the main open question.
2. **Seeds:** three. Seed SD is up to 0.36 dB, larger than most effects. `Te2` is inconclusive largely for this reason.
3. **Margins:** the equivalence margins were chosen by the authors (pre-registered, but not externally validated). Equivalence holds for this protocol only.
4. **Data:** synthetic, in-distribution degradations on one 256×256 face dataset. Images and references are stored rotated or flipped.
5. **Measurements:** training durations for seeds 42 and 123 are invalid (machine sleep), and inference peak memory doesn't separate the models.

## 8. Recommended next experiment (not started)

**Convergence pilot, `Tfull` vs. `Tnone`:**
- Setup: 256 px, 20k steps, 1 seed each, full 250-image validation every 1k steps.
- Estimated cost: about 4.7 h for `Tfull` and about 3 h for `Tnone`, run under `caffeinate`.
- Purpose: tests whether the "translators are unnecessary" result holds when training is longer and validation curves can be compared.
- If it holds, run the converged 3-seed study with fair baselines (Restormer, PromptIR, NAFNet), using `Tnone` as the main model.
