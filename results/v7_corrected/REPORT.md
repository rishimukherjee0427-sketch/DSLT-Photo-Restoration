# DSLT v7 Corrected-VGG Gating Ablation: Results Report

**Series:** DSLT-A / B / C, seeds 123 and 2026, trained with the corrected ImageNet normalisation for the VGG16 perceptual loss.

This is a **separate two-seed series**. It must not be pooled with the legacy seed-42 runs in `results/ablation/`, which used the un-normalised VGG loss.

The tables below are generated by `experiments/stats_v7.py` from `results/v7_corrected/per_image/*.json`. No number in them was typed by hand.

## 1. Protocol (measured settings)

| Item | Setting |
|---|---|
| Data | Trained on `data/splits/train.txt` (4,279 images); best epoch selected on val (24 images × 3 severities); tested once on `test.txt` (250 images × mild/medium/severe, cached deterministic degradations) |
| Training | 800 steps (8 epochs × 100), batch 4, crop 128, AdamW 2e-4, cosine schedule, warmup 50, grad clip 1.0, EMA 0.99, mixed severity, FP32, `vgg_normalize=True` |
| Configurations | A = ungated. B = adaptive FiLM gating. C = B + gate severity supervision (w=0.5) + sparsity (w=0.01). Identical seeds and data order within each seed |
| Metrics | PSNR (per image, capped at 50 dB), SSIM (skimage), LPIPS (AlexNet) |
| Precision | FP32. The 50-step profiler showed no AMP speedup on MPS (1.00–1.01×), and BF16 gradients diverged (cosine 0.17 vs. FP32); see `profile/` |
| Integrity | All 6 checkpoints passed (`checkpoint_integrity.json`): SHA256 matches the training manifest, all weights finite, metadata as expected |
| Compute | 4.0–4.1 min per run (6 runs, 24.7 min) plus 5.1 min test evaluation, on an Apple M4 Pro with 24 GB |

## 4. How to read the two kinds of evidence

- **Image-level evidence** (Wilcoxon p, Holm-adjusted) asks whether *these particular trained models* differ consistently across the 250 test images. It conditions on the two training runs. A small p means the difference is consistent across images for these checkpoints. It does **not** mean the difference would reappear with new training seeds.
- **Seed-level reproducibility** is shown by the per-seed Δ and the seed×image bootstrap CI, which resamples seeds as well as images. With only two seeds, this evidence is weak: seed-level SD cannot be estimated reliably, and a CI that crosses 0 means a finding is not established at the training-run level.
- **Practical threshold:** a difference counts as practically meaningful here only if it is at least 0.1 dB PSNR **and** seed-robust, meaning both seeds share its sign **and** the bootstrap CI excludes 0.

## 5. Findings

**Measured (established within this protocol):**
1. **Adaptive gating without supervision (B) does not help, and is slightly worse than ungated (A).**
   - Both seeds agree on a lower PSNR at mild and medium (Δ −0.12 / −0.15 dB).
   - SSIM is lower at mild and medium (Holm-significant).
   - LPIPS is worse at medium and severe (Holm-significant).
   - No B − A comparison favours B in a seed-robust way.
2. **Adding gate supervision and sparsity (C) recovers B's deficit.**
   - C − B is +0.10 to +0.11 dB PSNR at all three severities.
   - Both seeds favour C, and the gaps are Holm-significant at image level.
   - Only the medium-severity CI excludes 0 ([+0.033, +0.201]).
3. **C versus A shows no meaningful difference.**
   - **Mild and medium:** Δ PSNR −0.015 and −0.035 dB, not significant.
   - **Severe:** C is slightly better, with +0.071 dB PSNR and +0.0021 SSIM (image-level Holm p = 7e-05 and 2e-07; both seeds positive).
     - The PSNR gain is below the 0.1 dB threshold, and its seed×image CI includes 0 ([−0.021, +0.158]).
     - The severe SSIM CI does exclude 0 ([+0.0011, +0.0031]), but the effect is very small.
   - **LPIPS:** C is slightly worse than A at all severities, by +0.0011 to +0.0018. It is Holm-significant only at medium.

**Conclusion on adaptive gating:** within this budget, damage-conditioned adaptive gating provides **no meaningful improvement** over the ungated model.
- **Without supervision**, it slightly degrades quality.
- **With severity supervision**, it reaches parity with the ungated model, plus a small, consistent but sub-0.1 dB gain on severe damage only.
- **LPIPS** is never improved by gating.
- **Interpretability:** per the earlier diagnosis, the gates do track damage severity. Interpretability is therefore the defensible contribution, not accuracy.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Two seeds only.** The approved budget allowed 6 runs. Seed-level variance is poorly estimated, and the SDs above come from n = 2.
2. **Short training.** 800 steps at 128 px is far from convergence, so the conclusions may not hold for fully trained models.
3. **Validation subset.** Best epochs were selected on a 24-image validation subset. Most runs selected their final epoch (7 or 8), which suggests the models were still improving.
4. **Not comparable with earlier rows.** These runs used the corrected VGG normalisation, which makes the perceptual term about 2.9× larger at the same 0.01 weight. They therefore cannot be compared directly with the legacy seed-42 A/B/C runs or the Restormer/PromptIR baselines in `results/benchmark_table.md`, which all used un-normalised VGG.
5. **Synthetic data.** Degradations are synthetic, on 256×256 face images, and there is a single test split.
6. **Hand-set gate target.** The gate-supervision target mapping (a = 0.2, b = 0.8) is heuristic. It was not tuned, and per protocol no tuning was done on test.
